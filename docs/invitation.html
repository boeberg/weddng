Getting There

If you need help to plan the trip or join others taking the same trip, let us know, we are happy to help.

By Car

Coming from Berlin, the drive takes about three hours and covers roughly 225 km. The usual route goes via the A24 toward Hamburg, then the A14 and A20 in the direction of Wismar/Lübeck, followed by local roads toward Kalkhorst and Hohen Schönberg.

From Hamburg, the drive is much shorter at about 1 hour and 10 minutes. Take the A1 toward Lübeck, then the A20 toward Rostock, exit near Schönberg, and continue on local roads to Kalkhorst.

From Copenhagen, driving takes around seven hours depending on traffic and border crossings. The route goes via Denmark's E20 and the bridges to Germany, then south past Hamburg and east toward Lübeck and Kalkhorst. Driving is the easiest option for the final rural stretch.

By Train

Kalkhorst does not have a long-distance train station, so travel involves a combination of train and local transport. From Berlin, trains run to Lübeck, Wismar, or Schönberg via major hubs such as Hamburg or Bad Kleinen. Total travel time is usually five hours or more.

From Hamburg, regional trains run frequently to Lübeck, taking about 45 minutes. From Lübeck, continue by regional bus, taxi, or a short regional train connection toward Schönberg or Grevesmühlen, then on to Kalkhorst. Expect a total travel time of around three to four hours.

From Copenhagen, take a train to Hamburg, then continue to Lübeck and onward by regional transport. This journey typically takes seven to nine hours depending on connections.

By Plane

The most convenient airport is Hamburg Airport, which is about 80 km from Kalkhorst. From there, you can rent a car for a direct drive of about 1 hour and 15 minutes, or take a train via Hamburg Hauptbahnhof to Lübeck and continue by bus or taxi.
